Phase 1 — Weakness map and demand map

Lane: CREDIT · Model: 2 — Credit Risk · Phase: 1 of 6 Revision: 1 · Date: 11 November 2026 · Author: Sharmila Sirivaram Registration: CREDIT-RISK-P0-PREREG-001 (frozen 2026-11-11) · Evidence: phase1-risk/out/ Harness: phase1-risk/ — weakness.py · intervals.py Status: base UNMEASURED. Every weakness row below is either measured on the reference model, sourced from public literature, or marked UNMEASURED with a commission. None is a claim about a model that has not been run.

The one sentence. Phase 1 does not invent weaknesses; it names them, prices each one in the smallest difference that would count as real, and attaches the measurement that would prove it — and on this lane the base is unmeasured, so the honest map has an empty base column, a measured reference column, and a commission register where the claim would go.

0. Read this first

What Phase 1 is for. Phase 0 fixed what to measure. Phase 1 answers two questions: where does the model lose points that a buyer would pay to recover, and what would that buyer actually accept? The first is the weakness map; the second is the demand map. Together they decide what Phase 2 sources data for — because a mixture built around what the model already does well buys a few points near a ceiling and is named maintenance, not mission.

What this packet cannot say. It cannot say "the base is weak at calibration". The base has never been scored. Any sentence of that form in this document carries an UNMEASURED mark and a commission number. What it can do — and does — is measure the ground the base will stand on: the reference model's errors, the segment where the task is hardest, the interval around every difference, and the acceptance tests a paying customer would run.

The two maps, in one line each.

  • Weakness map: nine rows, of which two are measured weaknesses on Taiwan, three are structural findings (they describe the data, not the model), one is a measured non-weakness, and three are unmeasurable today and commissioned.
  • Demand map: five buyer archetypes, eight acceptance criteria, of which six can be measured on corpora we hold and two cannot — explanation and out-of-time stability. Those two are the difference between a model and a product a regulated buyer can sign for.

1. The rule this phase runs on

The Recipe's Phase 1 text is unambiguous:

Per benchmark: base score, why points are lost, the smallest improvement that counts as real (per-benchmark MDE), and the stretch target… Every claimed weakness needs a measured base number behind it, or an UNMEASURED mark and a measurement commissioned.

So every row in §3 carries three things: the evidence class, the measured number it rests on, and the MDE — the smallest improvement that is distinguishable from noise on that benchmark. A weakness without all three is not a weakness; it is a hypothesis with a test attached, and it is labelled as one.

Evidence class What it means Where it comes from
A — measured here produced on the frozen folds by a script in phase1-risk/ out/weakness.json, out/intervals.json
B — public literature / benchmark someone else's measurement, cited and labelled §7, §5
C — UNMEASURED, commissioned no measurement exists; a test is named and priced §10

The base's own row is class C in every table. That is not a placeholder: it is the honest state, and CM-R1 is the commission that ends it.

2. What the reference model is, and why it is allowed to stand in

The reference is the gradient-boosted control from Phase 0: HistGradientBoostingClassifier, seed 20261111, 300 iterations, fitted inside the frozen folds, scored out-of-fold so no row is scored by a model that saw it. It is not a contender and it is not the base. It is the floor of what the job can be done with, and its error structure is the best available proxy for where any model — including a 4 B decoder — will lose points.

Why that proxy is legitimate for Phase 1 and not for a verdict: Phase 1 is asking where the task is hard, not how good our model is. The task's difficulty is a property of the data. When Phase 6 asks how good the base is, the reference becomes the bar and the proxy stops being a substitute.

One caution, stated before the numbers are read. The reference sees 23 clean numeric fields and uses them well. A language model sees the same fields serialised as text. A weakness that is "structural for the task" (thin-file, imbalance, missingness) transfers; a weakness that is "structural for trees" (splits on thresholds) may not. §7 separates the two.

Read the table as a whole, not row by row. Two rows (W1, W2) are measured weaknesses of the task — places where a model that is good overall still loses the cases a lender cares about. Two (W4, W8) are structural: no amount of training fixes a model whose signal is repayment status, and no model can demonstrate stability on a corpus that has no time. Three (W5, W6, W8) cannot be measured on what we hold. And one (W3) is a measured non-weakness: on 30,000 rows the reference's calibration holds to within 3.3 points, which is the honest version of "we thought calibration would be bad".

4. The measured evidence

All figures are out-of-fold on the frozen Phase 0 splits; bootstrap 1,000 resamples, seed 20261111. Receipts: phase1-risk/out/weakness.json, intervals.json.

4.1 Where the defaults are missed

Decline rate Rows declined Defaults caught Defaults missed Recall Precision Lift
5 % 1,500 1,136 5,500 0.171 0.757 3.42×
10 % 3,000 2,093 4,543 0.315 0.698 3.15×
20 % 6,000 3,386 3,250 0.510 0.564 2.55×
30 % 9,000 4,187 2,449 0.631 0.465 2.10×

Taiwan, n = 30,000, 6,636 defaults. German is the same shape at a tenth of the size: 10 % decline catches 80 of 300 (0.267), misses 220.

Why this is a weakness and not a score. At a 10 % decline the model catches roughly one default in three. The other two are approved and then default. That is the operating reality of consumer credit — but it is also the row where a point of recall is worth more than a point of AUC, because the lender pays the loss, not the statistician. The interval is tight on Taiwan: 0.3154 [0.3080, 0.3229], SE 0.0039.

The low-limit segment holds 37 % of all defaults and the model is weakest exactly there: AUC 0.751 against 0.773 elsewhere, and within-segment recall of 0.227 against 0.388 in the highest band. The two intervals do not overlap (0.7386–0.7634 vs 0.7648–0.7810), so this is a real gap and not a binning artefact.

German shows the same pattern by age rather than limit: the 19–30 band scores AUC 0.736 against 0.783 (31–45) and 0.788 (46+) — the young, thin-file applicant is the hard case in both corpora, which is a property of credit data rather than of one dataset.

4.5 The fairness diagnostic, with its limits stated

At a global 10 % decline cut, by sex: male 11.24 % declined (default rate 24.2 %), female 9.19 % (20.8 %) → disparate-impact ratio 0.818, which clears the conventional 4/5ths screen. By age band: 31–45 8.84 %, 21–30 10.73 %, 46+ 11.84 % → AIR 0.747, which fails the same screen.

This is a diagnostic, not a verdict, and the packet says why: the decline rates track the base rates (46+ defaults at 25.2 %, 31–45 at 20.9 %), the corpus is one Taiwanese card portfolio from 2005, and the ratio is computed on a policy (decline the riskiest 10 %) rather than on a legally defined protected class. It is enough to put fair-lending evidence on the Phase 2 list; it is not enough to claim the model is or is not discriminatory.

5. The demand map — what a paying customer's bar actually is

A buyer of a credit-risk score does not buy AUC. They buy a decision they can defend to three audiences at once: their credit committee, their regulator, and a declined applicant.

Buyer What they are buying What they will actually test What that maps to here
Digital lender / BNPL a decision in milliseconds at application AUC on their own holdout; recall at the decline rate they can afford; a latency budget W1, W2, R6
Bank model risk (IRB-style) a PD they can put in a capital calculation calibration slope and Brier; use test; validation evidence; documentation W3, W8, §5.1 A3/A6
Risk operations an alert queue that is staffable precision at the operating point; stability week to week W2, W8
Compliance / legal a decline that survives an appeal reason codes on every decline; fair-lending evidence; licence provenance W6, W7
Platform / reseller something they can version and audit reproducible scores, pinned revisions, provenance receipts §5.1 A8

5.2 The finding the demand map produces

Six of the nine criteria can be measured with the corpora on disk. Two cannot be measured at all — A5 (explanation) and A6 (stability) — and one is diagnostic only (A9). A buyer in the first three rows of the table will notice: no regulated lender signs for a PD with no stability evidence, and no compliance function signs for a decline with no reason codes.

That is not a training problem. It is a data problem, and it belongs in Phase 2:

Gap What would close it Where it stands
No out-of-time corpus a loan-level set with issue dates and performance, licence-clear Home Credit has no application date; Lending Club has issue_d but an unproven licence; Freddie Mac needs an account
No explanation benchmark HELOC xML challenge, terms read and licence proven blocked (R-06)
No thin-file population a first-time-borrower or BNPL corpus, licence-clear none identified

The honest consequence. On the corpora we hold, this lane can build and prove a discrimination and calibration product for a lender with bureau data. It cannot yet prove an explanation or stability product for a regulated one — and those are the two things the lane's own Phase 0 said were the only defensible reason to use a language model at all. The demand map and the weakness map point at the same gap from opposite directions, which is the strongest signal either could give.

6. Where small models fail — the hypotheses, and the test that would prove each

The owner's question is where small models fail today. On this lane, the honest answer has two halves: what is known (literature, class B), and what is untested here (class C, with a named test). Nothing below is presented as our measurement.

6.1 What is known from public work

  • Trees still beat neural networks on medium-sized tabular data. Grinsztajn, Oyallon & Varoquaux (NeurIPS 2022, Why do tree-based models still outperform deep learning on typical tabular data?) is the reference result, and it is why the bar for a 4 B decoder is a GBM and not another decoder.
  • Serialisation matters for language models on tables. TabLLM (Hegselmann et al., AISTATS 2023) showed few-shot tabular classification with LLMs is feasible, and that the text template — not just the features — moves the result. That makes column order and number formatting a protocol variable on this lane, and Phase 0 froze them for exactly that reason.
  • Small-data tabular models are strong without training. TabPFN v2 (Hollmann et al., Nature, January 2025) is the current demonstration, and it is why TabPFN sits in the Phase 0 shortlist as a reference, not a contender.

These are class B: someone else's measurement. They set the burden of proof. They do not settle it.

6.2 What is untested here — seven hypotheses with their tests

# Failure mode Why it would bite on this lane The test that proves it State
H1 Serialisation sensitivity — score moves when columns are reordered or units rescaled the only interface to a tabular row is text permute field order and scale BILL_AMT* by 1/1000; AUC must move < MDE (0.014) UNMEASURED (CM-R6)
H2 Numeracy — comparisons and thresholds on numbers PAY_0 and LIMIT_BAL are the whole signal; both are numeric monotonicity probe: raising PAY_0 (worse repayment) must not lower PD; count violations UNMEASURED (CM-R6)
H3 Miscalibrated token probabilities P(default) is a softmax over two tokens, not a fitted probability gate G2: Brier, slope, intercept UNMEASURED (CM-R1)
H4 Imbalance collapse — everything predicted to the majority 4.8 % (Polish) to 30 % (German) base rates recall @ 10 % decline, Brier, and the capture table UNMEASURED (CM-R1)
H5 Missing values — NA handling neither headline corpus has a null, so a base can look fine and fail in production MCAR injection at 10 % and 30 % on Taiwan; AUC loss < MDE UNMEASURED (CM-R2)
H6 Unfaithful explanations — plausible reasons that do not describe the score the reason channel is the product's whole justification HELOC protocol, or attribution-agreement diagnostic (never a score) UNMEASURED (CM-R3)
H7 Unit economics — GPU cost per decision a GBM scores in microseconds on CPU measure rows/GPU-hour and cost per 1,000 decisions against the API price UNMEASURED (CM-R7)

H7 deserves its sentence, and it is measured on one side. The reference scores 443,842 rows/s — 2.25 µs per decision on one CPU core (intervals.json). The Phase 0 estimate for a 4 B model is 200–400 rows/s batched, so 1,100–2,200× slower per decision, and an order of magnitude worse single-stream. If the lane sells decisions, the model must be doing something the GBM cannot — and per §5 that something is A5, which is unmeasurable. That is the commercial form of the same gap.

7. What not to train — the strengths, named before Phase 2

The Recipe's warning is specific: a mixture dominated by what the base already does well buys a few points near a ceiling and is maintenance, not mission. Applied here, before any mixture exists:

Do not build a mixture around… Why
German Credit 1,000 rows; every model is within 0.06 AUC of every other; it cannot resolve an improvement (§Phase 0 power table)
The majority class at 22 % prevalence the "always good" baseline is already 78 % accurate and worth nothing
Taiwan's high-limit segment AUC 0.770 and recall 0.388 — already the easy end; it is a maintenance row
Aggregate AUC the reference gains most of its remaining error in the thin-file segment; aggregate AUC hides exactly that

And the corollary: a row is mission only if it has a measured weakness behind it (W1, W2) or a commission that will produce one (W5–W9). Everything else is maintenance and must be labelled so in the Phase 5 mixture, with its share capped.

8. What Phase 1 hands to Phase 2

The weakness map is a shopping list only if it names the shape of the missing data:

Weakness Data shape that would address it Track
W1 thin-file discrimination first-time-borrower / BNPL applications with outcomes; alternative-data fields A (public) or B (generated)
W2 recall at the operating point more defaults at the same prevalence, not more rows — the segment is data-poor, not row-poor A
W5 missing-data robustness a corpus with documented missingness (Polish, 41,322 ?) plus injected-missingness fixtures A + fixture
W6 explanation HELOC or equivalent with reason-code ground truth A or B, licence-gated

Coverage is judged against this table, not against row counts. A 300k-row corpus that adds no thin-file cases covers none of W1 or W2 and must be flagged as such in Phase 2, however large it is.
