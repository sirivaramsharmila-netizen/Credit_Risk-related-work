Phase 2 — Sourcing, the licence register and per-record provenance

Lane: CREDIT · Model: 2 — Credit Risk · Phase: 2 of 6 Revision: 1 · Date: 11 November 2026 · Author: Sharmila Sirivaram Gate: per-record provenance (Recipe v1, Phase 2) · Register: phase2-risk/register.json Evidence: phase2-risk/out/gate_report.json · slices_polish.json · candidate_laotse.json Status: 11 sources in the register. 5 admitted, 2 pending terms, 3 rejected, 1 out of scope. No corpus built.

The one sentence. Phase 2 is where a corpus becomes admissible, and for this model the arithmetic comes out badly: the two corpora that can measure a verdict are the two that must never train, the 43,405-row training corpus narrows to 3,194 rows and 30 bankruptcies once sliced, and the one clean new admission is synthetic — so the honest output of this phase is a training gap, stated with numbers, not a corpus.

0. What this packet covers, and the owner's instruction applied to Model 2

The owner's Phase 2 instruction is written for the fraud model: public fraud datasets only, each with a licence entry in a register and per-record provenance; published datasets are a permanently flagged class — labelled as dataset rows, never described as anything else. The same rule applies here with the domain substituted — public credit-risk datasets only — and this packet keeps every part of it:

Instruction How it is applied here
public datasets only every source is a publisher-hosted corpus; mirrors are a named rejection class (§5)
licence entry in a register 11 rows, each with the evidence line and the date it was read (§3)
per-record provenance a frozen stamp, applied at ingest, one row per record (§6)
published datasets are permanently flagged origin_class = DATASET_ROW on every record, forever; synthetic ones additionally carry synthetic = true and are never described as real applications (§7)

What Phase 2 is not. It is not the corpus. Nothing here has been ingested, split, decontaminated or trained on. Those are Phase 3, and they are gated on this phase's verdicts — which is why the negative result below matters more than the admissions.

1. The gate, in three predicates

A record ships only when its own per-record data names the upstream corpus whose content it is, and the licence is that corpus's licence. Dataset-level licence, mixture-card licence and disclosed generation method do not count. Per-record subset/category labels do count as origin.

Predicate Question Executed by
P1 origin can every record name its corpus, with a stamp applied at ingest rather than added later? gate.py — re-hashes the curated file, checks row counts, unique record ids and that a stamp is buildable
P2 licence does that corpus permit commercial training, with duties recorded? register evidence line, read at the publisher, dated
P3 coverage does it cover at least one weakness row from Phase 1? register coverage field, checked against W1–W9

Verdicts are fixed: ADMITTED · REJECTED · PENDING TERMS · OUT OF SCOPE. Pending terms is never admissible — an auth-gated licence that has not been read is an unknown, not a maybe.

One addition this model needs that the fraud model did not. A risk corpus can be perfectly licensed and still unusable for training, because it is the benchmark. So the register carries a role column — eval, train or none — and gate.py computes training-eligible rows from it. Admissible and trainable are different verdicts, and the difference is the finding of this phase.

3. The finding: a benchmark corpus cannot be a training corpus

This is the phase's central result, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion.

The lane's Phase 0 chose cross-validation for Taiwan (5 × 2 stratified folds) and the published 10-fold file for German. Under either protocol, every row is an evaluation row in some fold. If a model is fine-tuned on the corpus and then evaluated on those folds, it has already seen every row it is scored on — the evaluation measures memorisation, not skill. The Recipe's decontamination rule (zero benchmark examples in training data) therefore consumes the whole corpus, not a test split.

Corpus Role Rows Why
R-01 German eval only 1,000 official OpenML task 31, 10-fold
R-02 Taiwan eval only 30,000 lane protocol, 5 × 2 CV + holdout
R-03 Credit Approval train 690 admissible, but 1987 policy labels — not a PD
R-04 Polish train (corporate) 43,405 corporate distress, not consumer PD
R-08 Kaggle CC0 train (synthetic) 32,581 clean licence, synthetic applicant

Net evidence-grade consumer-PD training rows available today: zero. Every number that could train a consumer PD model is either synthetic, off-mission (corporate), or a 690-row table of 1987 policy decisions; every number that could measure one is reserved for evaluation.

That is not a dead end — it is a decision with three priced options (D-R11, §9):

Option What it costs What it buys
A. Keep both benchmarks as eval-only needs a new corpus (R-05 or R-06) before any training the strongest measurement (MDE 0.014 AUC) survives intact
B. Re-scope Taiwan to a single 80/20 holdout MDE rises 0.014 → 0.030 AUC; the 5 × 2 protocol dies and its receipt must be re-registered 24,000 rows become trainable immediately
C. Leave both eval-only and train nothing no trained model this cycle a zero-shot base row that is honest and measurable now

Recommendation: run C first — the Phase 0 base row is zero-shot anyway and costs one GPU-hour — and decide A vs B only if that row shows skill worth training on. Option B is always available and its price is now written down, which is the point of pricing it before it is chosen.

4. The one clean new admission — R-08, and exactly what it is

R-08 is a Kaggle dataset published under CC0 1.0, verified at source today: the dataset view endpoint returns CC0: Public Domain, and the file downloads anonymously (HTTP 200, zip 376,518 bytes). It passes P1 (per-record origin from row index and hash), P2 (CC0, no duties) and P3 (W5 — it is the only corpus on disk with real missingness).

It is also synthetic, and the evidence is in the values. No lender could have issued these rows:

Check Observed
Oldest applicant age 144 (5 applicants over 100)
Longest employment 123 years, in an applicant aged 22
Employment longer than the applicant's life 2 rows
Exact duplicate rows 165
Rows / defaults 32,581 / 7,108 (21.82 %)
Missing cells 895 person_emp_length, 3,116 loan_int_rate (9.56 %)

Under the owner's rule it is a published dataset and stays origin_class = DATASET_ROW forever, with synthetic = true on every record. It is admissible for training and not evidence-grade: a simulated applicant cannot certify a calibration claim about real borrowers, and a model trained on it inherits an age/employment distribution that no real portfolio has. Its honest use is narrow and useful — the missingness substrate for W5, and a robustness corpus, never a headline row.

Track C applied to it: 165 exact duplicates must be collapsed or family-grouped before any split, and the impossible-value rows (age > 100, employment > 60) should be quarantined rather than silently trained on. That is a slice decision, recorded here so Phase 3 inherits it.

5. Rejections, and the mirror class

ID Corpus Rejection Level Could it change?
R-06 Lending Club two mirrors, two licences (CC0 vs ODbL) for one upstream file licence only via LendingClub's own grant
R-09 Give Me Some Credit five re-uploads, five licences (Unknown, CC0, Apache-2.0, MIT, World Bank) licence only by reading the upstream competition terms
R-11 mirrors / re-uploads a label its publisher never granted origin never — the origin corpus governs

R-11 is the class, not a row. Observed today and previously: MIT labels on ULB mirrors, CC0 on Lending Club, a bare cc on HELOC. Under the per-record gate a mirror is not a source at all, because the licence belongs to the corpus, not to the person who copied it. Ingesting one would attach a licence the publisher never issued and put the same rows on both sides of a split.

R-10 HMDA is the one rejection that is not about licensing: it is public US government data, freely usable, and still useless here — it records application outcomes (approved / denied), not performance, so there is no default label to learn. It is the wrong target variable.

The 43,405-row corpus is a 3,194-row corpus with 30 bankruptcies once it is narrowed to one prediction target and rows a model can consume without imputation. Thirty positives is not a training set; it is a smoke test. The headline row count overstates the usable sample by roughly an order of magnitude, and the register now says so.

The limit this slice cannot test: there is no company identifier in the published file, so the same firm may appear in several horizons. Rows are not guaranteed independent, and a family split cannot be proven — the same defect the fraud lane found in ULB, in a different guise.

7. Track B — generated data, and why it cannot carry this model

The fraud lane can generate synthetic transactions and verify them by execution (a call executes, a ledger balances). Credit default has no execution semantics: nothing about a fabricated applicant can be run to prove it is a correct default. The recipe's Track B requires correctness by execution wherever the domain allows — here it does not allow it, and model judgement is explicitly not verification.

So the honest position is a narrow one:

Use of generated data Verdict Reason
The PD target (fabricate defaulters) Refused no execution check exists; a synthetic probability cannot certify calibration
Explanation / reason-code fixtures Admissible with receipts supervision is the fixture's own construction, not the model's opinion
Missingness and serialisation stress fixtures Admissible the property asserted (an NA was injected) is provable by construction
Additional real-shaped training rows Refused for v1 a generator's bias becomes the model's prior, and there is no way to audit it here

Any Track B row that is ever built carries origin_class = GENERATED, generator, seed, source_material and a verification receipt, and it can never be described as a published dataset — the same permanent-flag rule, pointed the other way.

8. Per-record provenance — the stamp

Frozen for this model, applied at ingest by the Phase 3 pipeline (not added later). It mirrors the fraud lane's stamp so one verifier can read both.

Field Value Why
row_id cr- + 9 digits, assigned in fixed source order then publisher row order stable identity across rebuilds
source_id german_credit · taiwan_default · credit_approval · polish_bankruptcy · credit_risk_laotse names the upstream corpus — P1
source_ref publisher URL the register row it belongs to
source_row_id pack record_id traceable to pack/curated/
source_row_index publisher row position the per-record locator
retrieved_at ingest timestamp (UTC) reproducibility
licence, licence_verdict copied from the register row P2, and it travels with the record
origin_class DATASET_ROW on every row; GENERATED only for Track B the permanent flag
synthetic true for R-08 and any generated row never described as real applications
benchmark_flag true where the row belongs to a frozen evaluation split keeps eval rows out of training
subset_label forecast_horizon_years for R-04 a per-record category label, which counts as origin
row_sha256 SHA-256 of the canonical token string the decontamination unit

Worked example (R-04, horizon 1):

{"row_id":"cr-000042317","source_id":"polish_bankruptcy","source_ref":"https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/365/polish+companies+bankruptcy+data",
 "source_row_id":"polish_bankruptcy-042317","source_row_index":42317,"retrieved_at":"2026-11-11T18:20:00Z",
 "licence":"CC BY 4.0","licence_verdict":"ADMITTED","origin_class":"DATASET_ROW","synthetic":false,
 "benchmark_flag":false,"subset_label":{"forecast_horizon_years":1},"row_sha256":"…"}

The rule that makes the stamp mean something: a record without a source_id that resolves to a register row with an ADMITTED verdict does not ship. P1_SOURCE_NOT_ADMITTED is a drop, not a warning.

Three gaps, one cause. W1, W6 and W8 need corpora this lane does not have: a real thin-file population, an explanation benchmark, and a dated portfolio. None of them is a training-mixture problem; all three are acquisition problems, and every one has a named candidate with a named blocker.
